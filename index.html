About me

I am a third-year Ph.D. candidate in Computer Science and Engineering at the University of Notre Dame, advised by Prof. Yiyu Shi. Previously, I earned my M.S. in Computer Science from Columbia University and my B.S. in Computer Science and Physics from the University of Michigan.

My research focuses on helping AI agents remember, reason, and make reliable decisions. I develop multimodal memory systems and evaluations, study decision-making under uncertainty, and build memory architectures for generation from long contexts.

I am seeking research or engineering internships in AI agents, multimodal learning, and large language models. I welcome research collaborations—please get in touch.

Research

Multimodal agent memory & evaluation

Studying how agents preserve visual details, track changing states, and retrieve the right evidence. My work includes MemEye and our survey of multimodal agent memory.

Reliable agent decision-making

Investigating when tool-using agents should ask for clarification, search for information, act, or escalate under uncertainty, balancing task success, interaction cost, and irreversible error risk.

Memory architectures & retrieval-augmented generation

Organizing long, noisy contexts into useful evidence. HM-RRG combines hierarchical memory with retrieved cases and longitudinal patient histories to improve clinical accuracy.
